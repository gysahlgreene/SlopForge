# Golden rig benchmark assets

**Checked:** 2026-10-06. Source models were downloaded to `/tmp` and inspected with Blender 5.1.0. The evidence below is a fixture inspection and partial import/deformation benchmark, not a Unity playback qualification. Keep source models outside product packages unless the exact asset and required notices are reviewed for redistribution.

## Recommendation

Use **Blender Studio Rain v3** as the first human rigging control: it is a purpose-built, functional Blender character rig with documented IK/FK and spine controls, a current Blender-compatible download, and explicit CC BY 4.0 terms. It is the best control for comparing deformation after sending a rig-stripped mesh copy through SkinTokens and Rigify. The official page does not claim to include animation clips, so make a small, named diagnostic action on the original rig rather than describing it as a supplied-clip test.

Pair it with the Khronos **CesiumMan** GLB for a supplied animated human import/export control, and **Fox** for a supplied multi-clip creature control. Those two are small glTF fixtures with per-model legal notices and explicit animation evidence. They exercise real source animations and Unity import separately from human auto-rigging quality.

## Local fixture findings

CesiumMan proves a key point: yes, correctly rigged examples materially help. The original weighted model and real clip provide a known-good control for mesh import, skin deformation, and clip extraction. It lets SlopForge tell an engine/export regression apart from a rigging-provider failure. It cannot prove an auto-rigger works, and a donor skeleton/weights cannot simply be attached to arbitrary generated topology.

Rain is the better visual/control-rig reference, but its complexity makes it a poor first provider input: 2,166 bones including 392 deform bones, facial controls, 18 separate mesh objects, and no supplied body animation. The source control rig is not yet visually benchmarked through its CloudRig UI because embedded script auto-execution was intentionally disabled. For the first provider comparison, create a disposable neutral, unrigged copy while preserving the original archive/hash, materials, and vertex positions; record whether modifiers, duplicate body variants, or hidden objects were included. Do not mix that comparison with voxel remeshing or SlopForge normalization.

These local files and renders are retained under [`docs/media/golden-rig-bench-2026-10/cesiumman`](../media/golden-rig-bench-2026-10/cesiumman/). The Rain archive is kept outside the repository under `/tmp/slopforge-golden-rig-bench/rain/` to avoid redistributing the source model.

## SlopForge provider probe — Rain neutral mesh

The official Rain ZIP was downloaded and opened with Blender 5.1.0 and `--disable-autoexec`; its embedded `cloudrig.py` and `rain_shading.py` were inspected but not run. A disposable unrigged GLB was exported from the visible evaluated geometry while retaining the original model and rig. It contains 18 separate geometry objects and is about 70 MB due to Rain's material/UDIM data; report hash `426269bf9f1079c68705256c36917ba0e02746ae7a8dc1fbafc2149987a4d280`.

SlopForge's unmodified Rigify provider rejects the exported mesh after welding because it treats `not edge.is_manifold` as a single error category. A separate BMesh probe classifies the 2,960 affected edges as boundaries and finds zero edges with more than two incident faces. In a throwaway copy that allowed boundaries but retained the other edge check, Rigify generated its 160-bone output. This proves boundary edges are not a structural prerequisite for its weight generation. It does **not** prove useful rigging: the contact renders show severely stretched facial and shoulder regions and poor joint quality. The provider's bounds fit, joint placement, and automatic weights still fail a professional character control.

The same neutral GLB loaded in SkinTokens at the reviewed H100 revision. Optional transfer failed because the upstream Blender helper connection closed while handling the multi-material/UDIM model. Without transfer, inference completed in 14.4 seconds and produced a fully weighted one-mesh result with 52 bones. SlopForge's postprocessor then rejected the skeleton because its semantic heuristic found zero candidates for the right upper arm. No pose renders, texture result, Unity import, or animation playback qualify this SkinTokens output. GPU peak memory was not measured.

Retained inputs/results and Rain-attributed renders are in [`rain-rigify-probe`](../media/golden-rig-bench-2026-10/rain-rigify-probe/). The archive and model binary remain in `/tmp`. The actionable conclusion is narrower than “use Rain's rig”: use known-good source models as separate controls, treat boundary shells distinctly from truly non-manifold edges, and keep provider prediction, packaging, semantic mapping, visual deformation, and engine playback as independent qualification stages. The immediate code fix should update the shared edge classification and the Rigify guard; preserving explicit human review and separate deformation/Unity gates remains essential.

## Recommended SlopForge use

1. **Keep a known-good rigged asset as the engine control.** CesiumMan is already a compact, verified animated GLB in Blender; Rain supplies a much more detailed authored human rest pose and source rig. Keep their licenses/attribution attached and never label either as a generated SlopForge result.
2. **Make provider benchmarks from rig-stripped copies, not the source model.** Hash the source and derived mesh, preserve the neutral geometry, and run every provider on the identical mesh. The Rain probe established this flow end to end, although its complicated materials prevented the optional SkinTokens texture-transfer path.
3. **Treat skeleton generation and usable character production as separate capabilities.** A candidate passes only after mesh cleanup, skinning, semantic bone mapping, meaningful diagnostic motion, materials, and Unity playback all pass independently. Current Rigify output fails visual deformation on Rain; current SkinTokens packaging fails semantic mapping on Rain.
4. **For the shortest route to a coherent animated game character, also benchmark a license-cleared pre-rigged base character with its matching animation library.** Quaternius Universal Base Characters and Universal Animation Library appear to provide this workflow and advertise CC0 for those dated packs, but archive notices and the site's later QAL must be recorded before adoption. This is a separate template/variant path, not evidence that arbitrary generated meshes can inherit the rig. Do not use Mixamo outputs as learned-provider benchmark input: Adobe's additional terms prohibit using its content to test or improve ML/AI systems.
5. **Do not transfer donor weights blindly.** Existing topology- and pose-specific weights are only directly reusable when mesh correspondence is preserved. If SlopForge changes topology, SkinTokens or another skinning provider must predict weights, followed by deformation QA; if preserving an authored mesh/skeleton, retarget its licensed clips instead.

This implies SlopForge should support two explicit character routes rather than forcing one rigging provider to solve both: (A) **rigged template/variant**, retaining a known skeleton/topology and compatible clips; (B) **generated-mesh rigging**, with independent mesh preparation, provider, semantic-map, deformation, and engine gates. Benchmark both against the same original and keep the current provider interface; the evidence does not justify selecting SkinTokens as default or replacing the existing character architecture yet.

## Benchmark protocol

Keep golden originals immutable and put disposable working copies outside the SlopForge asset library.

1. **Record inputs.** After a human downloads a chosen fixture, record source URL, retrieval date, file SHA-256, software versions, license notice, and an asset manifest. Do not treat the repository commit alone as the binary hash.
2. **Prove the source control first.** In the pinned Blender version, open Rain and play/render a short action keyed on its original controls (neutral, arms overhead, shoulder turn, elbow 90 degrees/deep bend, crouch, leg lift, knee bend). For CesiumMan and Fox, enumerate actual armatures, skins, materials, and animation names/ranges, then render their supplied clips. Save contact sheets and the Blender report.
3. **Test engine playback before provider comparison.** Export a disposable FBX from Rain with animation baking enabled; for glTF controls, import the original GLB through the selected Unity glTF importer and also test the Blender→FBX path. In Unity, verify scale/orientation, materials/textures, avatar/skeleton, bind-pose/skinned-renderer counts, clip names/ranges, and visible Animator playback. A successful import log is not a pass without frames playing.
4. **Create the provider input from the same rest-pose mesh.** Duplicate Rain's deform mesh at its neutral rest pose; preserve geometry, UVs, material slots, transforms, and a source hash. On the duplicate only, remove armature parenting/modifiers, vertex groups, and animation. Do not voxel-remesh, merge components, or rebake materials in this first comparison: those changes confound the rigging provider. If its input contract requires a single mesh or a particular rest pose, record a separate normalized fixture and run both providers on the same normalized copy.
5. **Run SkinTokens and Rigify in isolated provider environments.** Use identical input mesh copies and pinned provider/runtime revisions. Preserve raw outputs and logs. Capture the same eight diagnostic views plus neutral/T/A pose; measure connected components, mesh/material/UV retention, bone count/names, weighted-vertex coverage, max influences, normalized weight sums, and missing/extra objects. Review deformations visually against the source rig; structural checks alone cannot establish usable skinning.
6. **Separate rigging from animation compatibility.** Play the original Rain diagnostic action on its original rig in Blender and Unity as the known-good animation path. For the generated rigs, use a documented semantic bone map to retarget a short common clip only if the provider outputs support it; otherwise compare the canonical diagnostic poses and report retargeting as untested. Run CesiumMan/Fox supplied clips through Blender and Unity to qualify the import/export path independently.
7. **Promotion gate.** Do not select a rigging provider until an approved human has reviewed the contact sheet and the resulting Unity scene shows the expected animation without broken joints, slipping skin, missing textures, bad scale/orientation, or bind-pose errors. Preserve failing runs as evidence rather than tuning against a moving source asset.

This protocol addresses two separate questions: “Can SlopForge preserve and play a known-good rig/animation?” and “Can a provider skin the same clean unrigged mesh acceptably?” Passing the first does not validate SkinTokens/Rigify; passing Blender poses does not validate Unity playback.

## Not selected

Quaternius Universal Base Characters and Universal Animation Library are promising retargeting fixtures: their current pack pages advertise humanoid rigs and 120+/130+ animations, and link CC0. However, Quaternius also publishes a newer site-wide Asset License (QAL v1.0, 2026-08-28) that restricts standalone redistribution, while the pack pages still label the packs CC0. Resolve which terms govern the exact downloadable release and retain its included notice before relying on it; no download was made. See [base characters](https://quaternius.com/packs/universalbasecharacters.html), [animation library](https://quaternius.com/packs/universalanimationlibrary.html), [animation library 2](https://quaternius.com/packs/universalanimationlibrary2.html), and [current QAL terms](https://quaternius.com/license.html).
