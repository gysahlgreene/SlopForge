# 3D character rigging

Four-legged creatures are rigged by `slopforge creature rig` with generic names; see [Creatures](CREATURES.md#rigging-a-creature-b1). This guide covers humanoid characters.

**Current references:** [Humanoid test 1](media/character-qualification-2026-10/basalt-warden-unity-preview/README.md) demonstrates Unity walk playback; arm deformation and source topology remain under review. The [diagnostic humanoid benchmark](media/character-qualification-2026-10/basalt-corrected-humanoid/README.md) has approved body deformation and evaluated animation. Rigging providers remain experimental; broad deformation and production qualification are outstanding.

## Animation-readiness gate

### Source geometry review gate

Before approving structural readiness for a new rig, compare the retained mesh with the approved front, left, and rear design images. The source review is a separate human geometry decision. Blender's `front`, `side`, `rear`, and `three_quarter` renders are evidence of the candidate; they do not replace the three approved concepts. If the generation route used one image, the report records `single_image`; the review process does not turn it into multi-view conditioning.

Capture at least one concept crop and one close-up rendered from the retained Blender candidate for every concept-critical region. Review in neutral lighting and decide whether the modeled forms match the concepts before material appearance review. The selected model, approved concepts, four model renders, inspection report, every linked texture map, and close-up pairs are hashed. Missing or corrupt evidence leaves the report incomplete and cannot be approved.

```sh
slopforge --project ~/UnityProjects/MyGame character source-review moon_scout_character \
  --concept-view front=ai/references/moon_scout/front.png \
  --concept-view left=ai/references/moon_scout/left.png \
  --concept-view rear=ai/references/moon_scout/rear.png \
  --critical-region helmet \
  --concept-crop helmet=ai/references/moon_scout/helmet_detail.png \
  --model-closeup helmet=Assets/Art/Generated/Characters/moon_scout_character/Previews/helmet_closeup.png
slopforge --project ~/UnityProjects/MyGame review
slopforge --project ~/UnityProjects/MyGame character classify-source moon_scout_character <review-id> \
  --finding <finding-id>=intentional
slopforge --project ~/UnityProjects/MyGame character approve-source moon_scout_character <review-id> --confirm
```

Repeat `--critical-region`, `--concept-crop`, and `--model-closeup` for each region. The close-up must be a retained render of the same source model, not a material image. The report starts with component and topology findings marked `unclassified`; classify each as `intentional`, `repairable`, or `unresolved`. Reclassification is appended to manifest history. `repairable` and `unresolved` findings block rigging until the geometry is repaired and reviewed in a new evidence package. A disconnected component is not automatically debris; diagnostic positions and bounds are evidence for human classification, not proof or automatic repair.

Approval is tied to the full reviewed SHA-256 set. Changing the source model, concepts, preview, inspection report, close-ups, conditioning image, or material map makes that decision stale. Create a new review after changes. Source approval does not approve material appearance, alter asset readiness, clear topology findings, or establish deformation, performance, Unity playback, or pipeline qualification. Model and node identity, dependency versions, and license terms remain `unknown` when the retained provenance does not establish them.

The readiness report records structural measurements separately. It can be run before visual approval, but its approval cannot authorize rigging without a current approved source review. The source review also does not replace the explicit readiness approval shown below.

Run deterministic mesh inspection after approving a character model and before requesting a rig:

```sh
slopforge --project ~/UnityProjects/MyGame character readiness moon_scout_character
slopforge --project ~/UnityProjects/MyGame inspect moon_scout_character
slopforge --project ~/UnityProjects/MyGame character approve-source moon_scout_character <review-id> --confirm
slopforge --project ~/UnityProjects/MyGame character approve-readiness moon_scout_character
slopforge --project ~/UnityProjects/MyGame character normalize moon_scout_character
slopforge --project ~/UnityProjects/MyGame character approve-normalization moon_scout_character
slopforge --project ~/UnityProjects/MyGame character readiness moon_scout_character --source-output normalized_model
slopforge --project ~/UnityProjects/MyGame character approve-readiness moon_scout_character
slopforge --project ~/UnityProjects/MyGame character rig moon_scout_character --source-output normalized_model
```

The report records topology, connected components, transforms, face normals, bounds, UV coverage, per-mesh material and linked-image-texture assignment, missing texture files, auxiliary objects, Blender-world axes/units, source artifact, and source SHA-256. Missing or invalid geometry, UV, material, texture, coordinate, or helper measurements fail closed. `pass` still requires explicit approval; `needs_review` requires human approval after reviewing the model's orientation, intended scale, rest pose, and helper objects; `fail` cannot be approved. Rigging is blocked until the report is accepted. The manifest keeps model generation, animation readiness, rigging, deformation, and Unity Avatar states separate. This gate checks mesh structure and scale; it does not establish anatomy, useful skin weights, acceptable deformation, or Unity Humanoid validity.

The reported dimensions are in Blender units; the report also records the Blender scene unit system and scale and converts dimensions to metres for the size check. Unspecified scene units require human scale review. The recorded Z-up and -Y-forward values describe Blender's world basis, not an automatic judgment that the character faces the intended direction. Review those values against the front/side/rear views before approving readiness. A linked image texture is evidence that a texture node exists, not proof of useful coverage or visual quality; inspect the actual material in neutral lighting. A structural pass or human readiness approval does not make the asset game-ready: deformation review and Unity validation remain separate gates.

Readiness is classified from the raw measurements on every run and starts pending review. Approval and the shared rigging/normalization gate also check those measurements against the current contract. Older reports that lack the required fields cannot authorize processing, even when their saved status says approved; rerun `character readiness` and review the new report before approving it.

Each inspection uses a fresh temporary report and verifies that source bytes stayed unchanged while Blender inspected them. Missing output or a changed source stops the run and preserves the previous report and approval instead of treating stale evidence as current.

Material counts use slots actually assigned to faces. An unused textured slot cannot cover faces assigned to an empty or invalid slot. Linked-image checks establish node-link presence; human review must still verify the active shader and visible texture coverage.

Normalization is a separate, reviewable stage. It welds/remeshes components, creates UVs, and bakes available source material channels. All components share one voxel size from the combined bounds, so together they stay near `character_faces` instead of each part receiving the whole budget; a part smaller than about one voxel (roughly 1.3 cm for a 1.8 m character at 60k faces) comes out as a coarse blob. Bakes use the largest source texture size (1024 when a part has none), and empty material slots are recorded as `null`; inspect its report and compare it with the source before approval. Run readiness again against `normalized_model` and approve that report before rigging with `--source-output normalized_model`. The original generated model remains intact. Remeshing and baking can reduce visual fidelity; normalization cannot invent missing detail or repair a bad character design.

Rigging is a separate character stage; prop cleanup does not create a deformable skeleton. New projects include a first-class `character` model type and the `character_3d_pack` recipe. It generates one full-body neutral A-pose concept, runs the selected 3D model workflow, and records a reviewable model on a character asset. When the compute profile sets `workflows.character_image` (the H100 profiles do), concepts are conditioned on the OpenPose skeleton named by the character type's `pose_control` (`workflows/inputs/character_a_pose_openpose.png`: arms 45° down, hands clear of the hips). Prompt text alone did not hold the pose: an unconditioned stylized-hero brief gave hands on hips on both seeds tried, and a realistic brief arms about 20° out. With the skeleton both came out in the skeleton's A-pose. A stocky dwarf brief ignored it at every strength tried (0.65–1.6) and with stocky or T-pose skeletons, though its fists stayed clear of the belt. The skeleton's hash is part of the concept's conditioning identity. Character meshes use their own H100 graph when the profile sets `workflows.character_model` (the character type's `model_workflow`): `trellis2_image_to_model_h100_character_api.json` remeshes at 512 with QEF sharp-feature placement, projects vertices halfway back onto the generated surface, and decimates with QEM using crease-preserving quadrics. On the stylized hero (same concept and seed) the shared graph's 384 UDF remesh left serrated edges and spent about a third of the 60k budget on an inner shell that Blender later removed (41k faces delivered); the character graph delivered 53k visible faces with crisper armour edges and belt hardware. ComfyUI's `drop_inverted_components` was tried to remove the inner shell before decimation, but on another seed it deleted a shin and a hand, so the inner shell is still removed afterwards in Blender. A sampled reconstruction can also come out turned sideways (one realistic character, front-silhouette aspect error 0.69): character material candidates now fail with the retryable `concept_silhouette` code when the projection's aspect error exceeds 0.15 (good fits measure 0.02–0.035, stocky builds up to about 0.09), and approval tries the next seed. Experimental head pass (not a pipeline stage; its script `blender/graft_head.py` was removed and is recoverable from commit `f63d8cd`): a waist-up crop of the concept is refined image-to-image (Z-Image, denoise 0.35, which adds real facial detail while keeping identity; 0.5 drifted), reconstructed by the character graph (head-only crops of flat-shaded stylized faces came out as flat cards or reliefs in 4 of 5 seeds; waist-up crops were real 3D), placed on the body through concept pixel coordinates plus similarity ICP, cut under the jaw with an overlap and joined by an exact boolean union (self-intersection tolerant, materials transferred). On 2026-10-19 it clearly improved the realistic character's hair, beard and eyes (clean join), was mixed on the stylized hero, worse on the anime character and neutral on the dwarf; its joins on the stylized characters left hundreds to thousands of non-manifold edges. A 100k budget cracked the surface through repair, and a 2048 upsample with a 640 remesh added sculpted detail but blotchier texture and small holes, so neither is used. Configure `asset_pipeline.character_rigging_provider` as `blender_rigify` or `skintokens`. SkinTokens runs from a separately configured Python environment, checkout, and checkpoint; `character provider-setup skintokens` applies only the pinned, hash-checked compatibility edit to that checkout.

```sh
slopforge --project ~/UnityProjects/MyGame recipe run character_3d_pack --name moon_scout
slopforge --project ~/UnityProjects/MyGame candidates moon_scout_character
slopforge --project ~/UnityProjects/MyGame approve moon_scout_character 1
slopforge --project ~/UnityProjects/MyGame approve-texture moon_scout_character 1
slopforge --project ~/UnityProjects/MyGame character rig moon_scout_character --source-output normalized_model
```

Provider outputs can be recorded with `slopforge.character_rigging.record_rigging_result()` as typed artifacts on a `character` manifest asset. The result names an already approved source output, a project-relative FBX or GLB, optional PNG pose evidence, skeleton-name mapping, and provider provenance (`name`, `version`, `source`, and `license`). Registered rig and evidence artifacts remain candidates pending human review.

The recorder rejects paths outside the project, missing/empty exports, unapproved source artifacts, unsupported mesh formats, invalid evidence images, and incomplete provider license metadata. It records missing recommended pose evidence without pretending the rig passed deformation checks. Suggested evidence poses are T-pose, raised arms, crouch, leg lift, elbow bend, and shoulder rotation.

## Blender Rigify provider

With `blender_rigify` selected, `slopforge character rig <name> [--source-output model]` runs Blender's bundled Rigify add-on against a ready, approved GLB or FBX model artifact:

1. **Skeleton.** It uses Rigify's basic human metarig (spine, neck, head, arms, hands, legs, feet), without the full face rig and finger chains whose many small bones fell outside generated heads and hands.
2. **Joint placement.** Joints come from the mesh itself, assuming an upright A-pose facing −Y: horizontal slices find the gap between the legs (crotch), the arms beside the torso (arm clusters), and the torso centre line; elbows, wrists, knees and ankles are snapped to the local limb centroid so bones lie inside the body. When the arms cannot be separated from the torso (arms touching the body), it falls back to scaling the metarig to the mesh bounds and says so in the report.
3. **Weights.** Bone heat runs on a watertight voxel proxy, trying finer to coarser voxel sizes until every proxy vertex is weighted; the weights are transferred to the real mesh by nearest-surface interpolation. Raw generated meshes are open, multi-shell surfaces on which bone heat finds no solution, and a too-fine proxy keeps their inner shells as separate hollow layers.
4. **Clean-up.** Weights are capped to the influence limit and remapped to body deform bones. Forearm and hand weight is kept only on vertices connected to the arm along the surface within a limb radius, so clothing beside a hanging hand does not follow it. Faces that fuse a hand or forearm to the thigh, pelvis or spine (common where A-pose hands touch the body) are deleted and counted in the report.
5. **Evidence.** It renders six review poses from a three-quarter view with key, fill and rim light (rest, raised arms, crouch, forward leg lift, forward elbow bend, shoulder rotation), measures vertex displacement and exports an FBX with deform bones only. The report maps Unity Humanoid bones to those exported `DEF-` bones.

Rig and pose artifacts are registered as pending review; the command never approves them.

### Mesh preparation for characters

Character asset types require a closed (manifold) mesh. Raw image-to-3D characters are open, multi-shell surfaces, so mesh preparation runs `--local-repair`: it deletes components hidden inside the body (inner duplicate shells), then removes only the faces around non-manifold edges and pinched vertices and refills every hole, widening the area around holes that will not close. The rest of the surface is untouched. A full voxel remesh is used only if local repair fails, because it melts faces, pockets and folds into clay.

### Motion-capture preview

`blender/retarget_mocap.py` retargets a BVH or FBX clip onto the rig's `DEF-` skeleton (the bones exported to Unity) or a SkinTokens rig's Unity Humanoid bones, choosing the CMU mapping that matches the rig. Limb bones follow their source bone's world-space rotation from the actor's rest pose; feet and toes follow it from the actor's average foot orientation during that side's stance, because skeletons put the toe joint at different points (CMU at the ball of the foot, SkinTokens further forward and lower) and copying the ankle-to-toe direction held generated feet 12–40° toe-up and folded their boots; torso, neck, head and end bones follow it from the actor's average posture over one stride, because the BVH zero pose is a synthetic straight-spined T-pose and the actor's habitual posture (CMU 08_01: neck about 9° forward) made characters' heads jut. Mocap rest poses (usually T-pose) differ from generated A-pose characters, so limb bones (upper arm, forearm, thigh, shin) are first swung onto the source bone's rest direction, measured joint to joint because file bone tails are guesses. Torso, neck, clavicle and end bones are not swung: skeletons place those joints differently (CMU's clavicle rises 22° from mid-chest, a SkinTokens clavicle falls about 30° from the neck), and copying that direction made characters shrug through the walk; the clip is turned to face the character, scaled to its hip height, optionally kept in place, and one gait cycle (left foot plant to plant) is recorded for looping. Hands follow the forearms because mocap wrist data is noisy. `blender/render_turntable.py <walk.blend> <frames> --animate` plays that cycle at capture speed on a lit turntable with the lowest foot planted. After baking, three corrections keep the motion readable on generated bodies. The actor is turned to face along its own hip travel over the stride, not its rest facing (CMU 07_02 walks about 30° off it, which twisted the legs against the pelvis). Each arm swings outward by a constant angle when the actor's hang angle would drive the forearm and hand through the character's own hips or holsters (16–21° on the slimmer style characters, 4–7° on the dwarf; `arm_clearance_offset_degrees`). Each leg swings outward slightly when a foot would reach the midline (`stance_widening_degrees`), keeping the foot's world orientation. Humanoid finger chains get a relaxed curl (`relaxed_finger_bones`), because mocap wrists and fingers are not retargeted.

Game-authored clips can be used instead: pass a GLB or FBX library with `clip` set to an action name, for example the CC0 [Quaternius Universal Animation Library](https://quaternius.com/packs/universalanimationlibrary.html) (`Walk_Loop`, `Jog_Fwd_Loop`, idles, combat; Rigify `DEF-` names in UAL1, Unreal-style names in UAL2). Those skeletons are renamed to the CMU names the mappings use, and a `_Loop` clip (or `loop: true`) is treated as one whole cycle. For walk previews use the [CMU Graphics Lab Motion Capture Database](http://mocap.cs.cmu.edu/) clip **07_02**. Clips were compared on one detected stride: a natural walk straightens both knees in stance and bends the elbows moderately. 07_01–07_03 were the only candidates whose knees both reach 0°; 07_02 has a 1.13 s stride, elbows 5–45° and a slight forward lean. 08_01, used earlier, never straightens the right knee (15°), bends the elbows up to 66° and leans back; 16_15, 35_01, 35_02 and 39_01 keep the knees bent 7–18°, and 02_01 has no detectable stride; CMU states the data is free for all uses and may be included in commercial products but not resold directly. Clips are downloaded into the ignored `private/` directory, not committed.

The provider uses the installed Blender executable and its bundled code; no extra Python package or model weights are installed. The provider reports Blender/Rigify version and GPL-2.0-or-later provenance. Review the installed Blender distribution's notices for the exact bundled add-on terms.

This is a landmark heuristic for upright A-pose humanoids, not general-purpose automatic rigging: unusual anatomy, props or capes, crossed or touching limbs, and non-humanoid characters need review or another provider. Deleting fused contact faces leaves small openings where hands touched the body. Inspect the source, rig and all six pose renders before approval; pose images and nonzero displacement do not establish acceptable deformations or Unity Humanoid compatibility.

SkinTokens is also available as an opt-in provider. It uses a separately configured runtime and checkpoint and remains experimental. To run SkinTokens on a GPU host, set `tools.skintokens_host` to an SSH host (key-based, non-interactive) and give `skintokens_python`, `skintokens_checkout` and `skintokens_checkpoint` as absolute paths on that host, typically in the GPU compute profile together with `character_rigging_provider: skintokens`. Preflight, `character provider-setup skintokens` and the seeded launcher then run there from the same audited `slopforge/skintokens_runtime.py`, streamed to the host's SkinTokens Python; the source is uploaded to a temporary directory, and the output and log come back to the project (`ai/logs/skintokens/`). The launcher drops HTTP proxy variables, because `demo.py` talks to a local Blender helper over HTTP and a host proxy intercepted it. SkinTokens decodes with sampling, so runs differ; the provider passes `--use_postprocess`, which masks predicted weights by voxel reachability from each joint. Without it, one of two runs on the same realistic character weighted about 1,000 trouser vertices to a fingertip (dominant bone 0.59 body-heights away), and the legs tore in the walk; two postprocessed runs had no such vertex. The post-processor then removes weights that jump across the skeleton: a patch of vertices led by one bone whose every mesh neighbour is led by a bone more than five steps away takes its neighbours' weights, and each vertex drops influences more than five bones from its dominant one (`relabelled_isolated_vertices`, `pruned_distant_influences`). Hands hanging against a holster or belt otherwise tied those parts to a fingertip, and they tore into long spikes when the arms lifted. With `--use_transfer`, SkinTokens keeps the source surface and UVs; `blender/skintokens_character.py` then reuses the source material as-is (`material_transfer: reused_source_material`) and only falls back to a selected-to-active rebake when UVs differ, because the rebake missed rays on folds and pockets and left dark dots. The post-processor renames the body chain to Unity Humanoid names (`Hips` … `RightToes`), and the provider records those bones as the rig's `unity_humanoid_mapping`, so `animation unity-setup --rig-type humanoid` accepts a SkinTokens rig; finger chains (`LeftFinger1` …) and `Extra_` bones are left out. A failed post-process reports the Blender script's own errors. The SSH work directory is accepted only as `mktemp`'s `/tmp/slopforge-skintokens-*` line, so start-up output from the host's shell cannot reach the cleanup `rm -rf`. Each output still needs source review, deformation review, and the intended engine import check; provider completion and structural measurements do not qualify a rig.

Character readiness checks flag models with excessive connected components, boundary edges, or non-manifold geometry for review. These checks do not repair geometry or prove good skinning, deformation, or texture coverage.

## Characters built in parts

A character generated as one model spends little of its mesh and texture on the head. On a long-coated test body (2026-11-01) the head had 3,158 of 46,732 triangles and 1.9% of the texture sheet, and the owner rejected the face even after repainting it from eight angles. The clay head was usable, so the loss was in the texture stage. Building the head as its own model fixed it. Building the body with no head also stopped the model inventing hair and left the collar whole. `processing/character_parts.py` runs the route, one step at a time, because the results need a look between steps. Of six head builds, two collapsed and one was clearly best, so build several and review them.

The steps read a JSON brief: `name`, `person`, `their`, `they_are`, `who`, `eyes`, `hair`, `clothes`, `surfaces`, `style` (the art style, which every painting prompt repeats, such as "An oil-painted look with hand-painted textures. Not a photograph, not a 3D render."), `height`, `concept` (the approved concept, which sets the head's size), and optionally `long_skirt`, `grade` (RGB gains on the body's non-brass colour), `head_base` (the repainted head colour map from `hair`, which `join` needs) and `body_base` (a repainted body colour map), `stump_neck` (how the edit leaves the neck), `back_of_head` (how to paint the back of the hair or head covering), `body_smooth`, `leg_swing`, `arm_drop`, `keep_contacts`.

```sh
python processing/character_parts.py images brief.json --out parts --head-source crop-of-approved-face.png --body-source approved-a-pose.png
python processing/character_parts.py build brief.json --out parts/head-6 --part head --image parts/head-6.png --seed 444555666
python processing/character_parts.py build brief.json --out parts/body-3 --part body --image parts/body-3.png --seed 444555666
python processing/character_parts.py hair brief.json --out parts/hair --head parts/head-6   # then set head_base in the brief
python processing/character_parts.py paint brief.json --out parts/paint --body parts/body-3   # only if the body's own colour is poor; then set body_base
python processing/character_parts.py join brief.json --out parts/joined --body parts/body-3 --head parts/head-6
python processing/character_parts.py rig brief.json --out parts/rig --joined parts/joined/character_joined.glb
python processing/character_parts.py walk brief.json --out parts/walk --rig parts/rig/character_rigged.fbx --motion library.glb --clip Walk_Loop
python processing/character_parts.py unity brief.json --project ~/UnityProjects/MyGame --rig parts/rig --joined parts/joined
python processing/character_parts.py gear brief.json --project ~/UnityProjects/MyGame --clip-library AnimationLibrary_Unity_Standard.fbx --pack Assets/Art/Generated/Models/rack/rack.fbx --weapon Assets/Art/Generated/Weapons/launcher/launcher.fbx --pack-scale 0.82 --pack-height 0.66
```

- **`images`** repaints the concept with `workflows/image_edit_qwen_api.json`: head close-ups in flat light, and bodies with only the head taken off at the neck. Erasing the neck too, in flat light, built a hollow shell with a void at the collar and lost the concept's colours. Compare each take with its input. To keep several characters in one style, make each one's concept an edit of the first character's ("keep this exact picture's art style, replace only the person"): generating them separately from text drifted between looks. The edit model takes prompts literally: naming a thing to leave out primes it ("no mask" painted a thicker mask), "clear irises" gave blank eyes where naming the colour worked, and full denoise gave better faces, because lower denoise keeps the input's flaws. Start from a concept with cleared rights, not from Qwen Image 2.1 art: that model's licence is research and evaluation only and doesn't clearly free its outputs.
- **`build`** is the normal TRELLIS.2 character graph at 1536 shape resolution and a 4096 sheet. It skips the material check, so one body came back 98.5% metal.
- **Choosing a head take:** look at it as plain clay before anything else, because no later step can repair a torn surface. Fine loose hair over a face tears into shards in the 3D step, and cuts through the eye sockets: one head with a fringe looked far worse than the same woman's head with her scarf tied tight. Three takes with no fringe gave sculpted faces.
- **`hair`** repaints the built head from flat renders of itself, front and sides for the face and behind for the hair, and projects the paintings back. A front-on concept projected onto a sculpted head doesn't line up: one head's painted eyes sat further apart than its sculpted ones, and the mismatch looked like the face warping whenever a pose turned the head. A render of the model is already aligned, so its repaint is too. The render uses the model's own colour in the concept's tone (`aligned_colour`), because that colour sits on the shape, though alone it stares and goes blotchy at the hairline. The 3D step also invents what a front concept can't show, and one head's back came out bald. Paintings blend by each texel's angle round the head, with one holding until a few degrees either side of halfway, and the back fades in across the middle over a face that reaches past it. Choosing one painting per texel by the generated normal, which flips strand by strand, tore the hairline and the neck into flecks, and a hard split at the middle left a line down the side of a leather cap. The back of a head takes its whole painting: a test for which pixels were grey hair left flecks at the nape and raw colour through thinning hair. So the back paintings must match the front, and `back_of_head` says how: asking for thick combed strands drew feathered hair and a bald patch on a man with a cropped front. The brief's `eyes` line sets the expression.
- **`join`** softens the head's normal map to half strength (`HEAD_NORMAL`), because at full strength it carved hard rings round the eyes, and scales the body to its share of `height`, measured from the concept and its edit, and cuts the head inside the stump. It welds the head first, because a GLB splits vertices along UV seams and each seam otherwise reads as a hole. It puts both on one sheet: the body in the bottom left two thirds, the head in the top right third.
  - The head is cut across its triangles inside the stump, with no cap. Deleting whole triangles left a torn edge.
  - Only brass, found by colour, is metal, and everything else has a roughness of at least 0.75, because leather at 0.49 read as PVC.
  - Surfaces facing away from the concept's camera keep the model's own voxel colour, because the front-on painting leaks buttons and background round the sides. It isn't matched to the front's: with a dark apron in front, that raised a shirt's back and the arms beside it.
  - A head's reduction holds on to its face. A plain reduction spends triangles where the surface is busiest: one man's goggles and cap kept most of them and his smooth face went from 4,667 to 1,009, a low-poly mannequin. In Blender's Decimate a lower vertex weight holds harder, and 0 never reduces at all, which starved the cap: 0.9 on the face is the setting.
  - A head's sheet is laid out again after the trim, with the face's islands drawn 2.2 times wider, and its colour and normal maps baked across. The sheet from the 3D step still held the cut bust and shirt, and busy surfaces split into thousands of islands: the packer's default margin grows with each island, so use a fixed one (`margin_method="FRACTION"`). The faces of the two worst heads went from about 5% of the head's sheet to about 20%. This costs no new 3D build.
  - A head is sized to the concept by one measure on both: the median width across the rows just under the crown. Taking the model's widest point counted a scarf's tail and loose hair as skull, which made all of three heads tested 5 to 14% too small and sat one chin inside its stump.
  - The body takes its skin in the repainted face's tone, keeping its light and dark (`match_skin`). The generator paints skin a saturated orange wherever the concept doesn't reach, and the stump's top and inner wall yellower still, while the edit leaves a neck paler than the face. The skin is found in the generator's colour, whose tone is even, and brightness is anchored on the neck, which meets the face. Anything else of that hue and brightness takes the tone too, so check tan trim.
  - The stump is cut along a plane rising from under the chin to the nape, and the head's neck is widened to the cut if a gap would show, and tucked inside the stump's outline under the cut, fading out to just above the stump's top. A neck runs wider and deeper than a stump, and its cut edge hung outside the collar with its shirt, while pulling rows in at one height stretched teeth to the row above. Measure against the stump, not the ring its cut leaves: the slope clears the stump's top at the back, so the ring is only the front of the neck. The jaw above the slope isn't touched: narrowing it pinched a face. The cut is capped with a fan of triangles in one colour, the ring's median: ring points can sit on different UV islands, and a triangle spanning two sampled the sheet between them, which showed as dark slivers under the chin.
- **`paint`** repaints the body from renders of itself, as `hair` does the head. It leaves out the straight-behind view, because the edit model paints the front's apron on the back. Paintings are blended by the angle round the body among the views that can see each texel, leaving out the grey backdrop. Choosing one view per texel by the generated normal laid streaks of two different coats side by side, and with no test for what a view could see, a fist printed itself on the coat behind it. Use it when a body's own colour comes out blotchy or chewed. It leaves a headless body's bare neck alone, because the paintings show an empty collar and have no neck to give, and only grey reaching the edge of a painting counts as its backdrop, because a highlight on leather can be the same grey.
- **`body_smooth`** in a brief takes the generator's small lumps out of the body's surface in `join` (Taubin smoothing, 20 passes on one coat). Unlit, a repainted body can look clean while lit it looks chewed, because the lumps catch the light and no longer match the paint. The join rebuilds the normals a glTF import stored and drops the body's normal map, both of which carry the lumps. There it softened the coat and its back's X-shaped marks, but buttons stay lumpy with small chips of brass, and dark marks stay high on the back.
- **The neck comes from the image edit.** Five repairs on one built figure failed (painting the bare neck as the shirt, a lower cut, slimming the stump, clean skin variations). Rewording the edit (`stump_neck` in a brief, or `STUMP_NECK` by default) fixed most of it. Compare the source image with the concept before repairing a built model, and compare chin heights before moving a head. The owner's verdict on that build is "still not right": cracks across the shirt, a torn nape and a ragged flap across the back remain.
- **`rig`** runs the Rigify provider below with `long_skirt` from the brief and always weights through the solid proxy (`solid_only`). `keep_contacts` skips cutting faces where a hand touches a thigh, for a low-detail body where that cut opened holes at the armpits. Its report stays review-only (`topology_policy: allow_for_review`), because the neck base keeps a few open edges inside the collar.
- **`walk`** retargets a clip for review. Judge colour with Blender's Standard view transform: the rigged blend's AgX washes it out.
- **`unity`** copies the FBX and sheet into the project and builds the material and Humanoid prefab. It refuses an existing folder, because a stale `.fbx.meta` keeps the previous skeleton.

- **`gear`** imports a Humanoid clip library once for the whole project, applies the brief's `leg_swing`, then mounts a pack on the chest and a weapon in the right hand as `<name>_equipped.prefab`. Clips made for trousers stride about a metre, far past a skirt, and a boot then floats free of the hem. `leg_swing` scales the swing of the hips, ankles and knees round the idle clip's stance through the Avatar's limits, so every clip takes smaller steps, keeping her stance. `[0.5, 0.55]` was right for the first long skirt and `[0.6, 0.6]` for two more characters. `arm_drop` scales how far the arms can lower, which keeps a big man's arms out of his torso: 0.5 on the first. An Animator state must keep Foot IK off, its default, or the clip's foot goals undo it. It also levels the head, by retuning the neck's and head's nod in the Avatar, so it reaches every clip with no game code. Unity straightens a neck by how its bones lean at rest, so one idle clip faced two generated figures 14 and 16 degrees down, with the eyes in shadow, and two others 8 and 21 degrees up. On the first it went from -14.0 to -0.1 degrees with the feet within 2 mm. The script's Animator must be set to `AlwaysAnimate`, because off screen it writes no transforms and the pose read back is the rest pose. The clips play on any character through Unity's retargeting. The CC0 [Quaternius Universal Animation Library](https://quaternius.com/packs/universalanimationlibrary.html) has 46 of them: on the first character, before `leg_swing`, feet stayed flat in idle and within about 2 cm of the floor walking, the same as its own mannequin, so binding in A-pose is fine. With `leg_swing` the lowest point of the mesh stays within about 3.5 cm of the floor walking. A check in Blender is not enough: the same clip through Unity's retargeting has a longer stride, and it floated the boots on a rig that had looked fine in Blender.

Weapons and packs are separate models, attached afterwards, so a body's concept shows empty hands and nothing on the back. Two limits came up on the first character. The `weapon` type needs Florence-2 and SAM 2.1 for its grip mask (`tools.parts_python`), and without them a weapon can be built as a `prop`. Publishing a prop drops it on a floor, and a long weapon tips until its pivot is below the floor and fails `RestsOnTheGround`, so such a model goes in through `build_unity_material` from its candidate folder. The basic human rig also has no finger bones, so a hand can't close on a handle: the grip sits in an open glove, which holds up at game distance but not in close-up. Packs have no straps yet.

A two-handed weapon has no clip in the library, and a weapon parented to one swinging hand drags the other arm across the body. `slopforge.unity_gear.two_hand` mounts it on the chest instead and bakes each clip with both arms solved onto the haft, frame by frame, as ordinary Humanoid clips any Animator plays, with no game code: `<name>_twohand.prefab` and `TwoHand/<name>_TwoHand_<clip>.anim`. It aims the middle of the closed fist at the grip, and damps the solve, because a fist's offset depends on the arm's angle and an undamped solve jumped between two answers 7 cm apart. Grips are set a fist clear of the body's front at the lowest height both arms reach, because a grip set from shoulder width ended inside a big man's belly. A bake stops if a fist ends more than 5 cm off its grip, if a forearm goes more than 5 cm into the body, or if a grip isn't on the weapon: a weapon whose origin sits at its grip, as the weapon route's do, was once placed 32 cm below the fists while they read as exactly on their grips.

Baked clips need `loopBlendPositionY`, `loopBlendPositionXZ` and `loopBlendOrientation` on, as the library's have. Without them the hips' rise and fall leave as root motion and are lost with root motion off: hips moved 1.6 cm against the library's 27.5 cm, and the owner called the result "extremely weird". The chest then turns against the hips, as a runner's does, and the lowest foot is held near the floor, because narrowed legs floated this man's foot 22 cm on a jog. Verified with one hammer on one character: both fists within 1 mm of their grips in playback, lower foot off the floor by 1.6 cm on the walk and 4.8 cm on the jog, and the weapon moving 9 and 32 cm over those clips. The owner on that motion, 2026-11-04: "definitely better". Judge motion by comparing hips, feet and frame jumps with the library clip and by consecutive side frames: fists on grips and turn angles alone passed all of these faults.

`two_hand_strike` adds an attack, after `two_hand`: `TwoHand/<name>_TwoHand_Attack_Swing.anim`, a swing from the side. It is computed, because the library has no two-handed swing. The swings checked, in it and in the free part of Quaternius's second pack, use one arm, an empty hand's wrist says nothing about a haft (a chop taken from one never came down), and the sword clips throw a heavy man's body 45 degrees forward. The weapon follows an arc round the middle of the shoulders, tipped over by `roll`. The weapon's curves are in the clip, under the chest where the hold mounts it, and the clip starts and ends on the hold, so a controller needs almost no blend and clips without those curves put the weapon back. `HAMMER_SWING` holds the shape and timing. What it taught:

- **The blow carries through.** Sped up to the blow and stopped there, the hammer looked to hit an invisible wall. Curves through five keys keep their speed through the blow and rest only where they turn back. The hips lead, then the spine, the arms and last the hammer, and the leads fade in from nothing so the clip leaves the hold exactly.
- **The body commits.** The front foot steps in and lands just before the blow, the back heel lifts as the body turns, and the weight crosses from back foot to front.
- **The arms move for themselves.** Shoulder blades follow the arms, elbows run from deep at the top to nearly straight at the blow (left bend 23 to 127 degrees, right 23 to 114), and each elbow flares on the side carrying the load.
- **A fist turns slowly on a round haft, and the forearm's twist is unwrapped and capped.** A side swing's haft has to cross the line of the forearm. Rolled at once from the forearm, hands flipped 175 degrees in a frame. Held rigid on the weapon, wrists bent to 141 degrees, where a real one stops near 80, and the forearm's twist muscle wrapped at 2, which spun the bone 175 degrees with the hand steady. Changing the path only moved these. A fist now turns towards the easiest wrist at 12 degrees a frame, its roll coming back to the hold's by the end. The twist is unwrapped from the last frame and capped at 1.9, and the arms then reach for the haft again, because the cap moves the fist. Shoulder blades ease too, at 5 degrees a frame, after one swung 52 in a frame.
- **Reach is measured at the wrist.** A fist's offset doesn't always point along the forearm, so the weapon comes in until both wrists can reach.
- **The top hand slides down for the blow, as on an axe.** Hands are placed from the shoulders, so moving the hips changes nothing, and with the hands apart the lower one ended inside a big man's chest.

A bake stops if a fist ends more than 5 cm off the haft, measured after the Humanoid round trip, if a forearm goes more than 5 cm into the body, or if a forearm, hand or shoulder blade jumps more than 60 degrees in a frame, and says which and when. Fists stayed on the haft through every flip, so nothing else shows those. An overhead shape with its forearm 6 cm into the body stopped it, and so did the flips.

Verified on one character with one hammer, through an `AnimatorController`, standing and over a jog with arms and spine masked: fists within 1.6 cm of the haft between the clip's frames as well as on them, largest joint jump under 30 degrees, forearm 3.4 cm into the body at most, length 0.88 s, the weapon back at its hold afterwards. Wrists may still bend past a real one's range: about 100 degrees on the left and 120 on the right on an earlier bake, not re-measured on this one. Drawing the hands in close for more elbow bend put the fists on his jaw and crushed the arms across his chest, so the wind-up stays out in front of the shoulder, with the elbows bending to about 124 degrees at most. The owner's verdicts ran "doesn't look too natural yet", "closer but the arms still look stiff", "still a bit off", then on this version, 2026-11-04, "swing looks good to me". That judges this one swing on this one character by eye. Not checked: any other figure, a walk underneath it, and playing it in a game. A game has to play these clips through its own controller or an override, and any hit timing it keeps must allow for the blow landing 0.40 s in.

A weapon carried ahead rather than across the chest takes a shape: `two_hand(..., carry=RIFLE_CARRY)`, `LAUNCHER_CARRY` or `WRENCH_CARRY`. The shape names the muzzle end, each hand's distance along the weapon from its origin (measure the mesh's sections first), the weapon's line, and which way each fist faces. The hammer's hold and swing are unchanged by this.

- **The weapon keeps most of its line while the body moves round it.** A jog leans the chest about 30 degrees and turns it about 15 each way. Carried on the chest, the rifle's muzzle swept about 60 cm and pointed at the floor, so each clip carries the weapon's own curves and the weapon takes 30% of the chest's turn. The prefab's hold is put back after the bake.
- **Each fist's way round is given, not taken from the forearm.** The forearm runs close to the barrel's line, where a roll taken from it has no answer.
- **A long gun can't point straight ahead.** One figure's arm reaches 0.64 m and the hands are 0.44 m apart, so the muzzle goes low and across the body.
- **The right elbow's way is in the figure's axes.** Turned with the chest, it sent the forearm 10 cm into the coat on the jog. A long arm folded on a close grip tucks the elbow into the ribs (17 cm on one walk frame) and, sent too far out, reads as a chicken wing. The cure was a hand further from the shoulder.
- **`one_hand` in a shape leaves the left arm to the clip.** `WRENCH_CARRY` uses it, and nothing measures that arm against a grip.

A bake stops if a grip is past the arm's reach, if the weapon goes more than 3 cm into the body, or on the arm and fist checks above. All three stopped a real fault on these weapons.

`two_hand_shot` adds a firing clip, `TwoHand/<name>_TwoHand_Attack_Shot.anim`: the weapon kicks back along its line and its muzzle lifts, the spine gives a moment later, and the clip ends on the hold. **Rig faults this route found, all fixed in `blender/rigify_character.py` for every Rigify character:**

- Breast bones took heat from the chest and upper arms and `cap_influences` gave that weight to the head, so the chest followed the head. They no longer deform.
- The limb radius measured one of the forearm's two twist bones, under 5 cm on a 2 m figure, which stripped forearm weight from a sleeve's surface and left it on the upper arm. It now measures the whole forearm.
- A torso slice taken while one arm was still merged with the body was measured across that arm, which stripped arm weight from a band of sleeve. A slice now needs an arm clear on both sides.
- Clearing a loose piece's groups through a list of `vertex.groups` items skipped some, because removing one moves the rest.

Sleeves on that body drifted up to 17 cm off the arm in a walk before these fixes, and 1.5 cm after.

**Found on the next three bodies, also fixed for every Rigify character:**

- `fit_landmarks` looked for gaps between raw vertices, and a sparse mesh has false gaps, which put the arm bones in the wrong place. It now reads even samples of the surface (`surface_points`). The report gives `arm_vertices`, and warns when the arms are lopsided or under 0.5% of the vertices.
- The hand was the far end of the largest arm-side piece, and a coat flap could win, which cracked the arm open. It is now the middle of the outermost 3% of height on that side.
- An apron on the centre line hid the crotch from the front, so the hips fell back to their default height. The crotch is now found from behind only. Hip width ignores anything past the arm's inner edge, because hands pulled the joint outwards.
- Loose pieces attached to the largest piece, which could be the head, so scraps of a low-detail body flew with it. They now attach to the nearest of the pieces over 5% of the vertices.
- `tube_skirt` finds the hem by coverage: the lowest height where the skirt still goes most of the way round.

**Check in Unity, not only in Blender.** Unity's retargeted stride is longer than the Blender retarget, and Blender's renders here have no self-shadowing, which hid the collar void completely. In batch mode set the Animator to `AlwaysAnimate` and warm up with Evaluate, `cam.Render()`, Evaluate after `SetTime`, or the clip doesn't apply and the figure stands in A-pose.

**Added for these bodies:**

- **Solid proxy.** A voxel remesh keeps a hollow body hollow and broke up on its inner layers (558 pieces), so bone heat found no solution. When no voxel proxy can be weighted, `solid_proxy` fills the body by flooding from outside and weights that instead.
- **`long_skirt`.** Bone heat gives each side of a skirt to its own leg, and it shreds as they part. With this request option the skirt follows the hips and both thighs as a tube down to its hem, each side favouring its own thigh. It is an option because it would stiffen trousers. Generated bodies have legs inside the skirt, fused to its pleats, and two simpler versions failed. Locking everything above the hem to the skirt tore the boots at that line. Freeing the whole leg tore the pleats and pushed knees through the apron. So only the boot goes with the leg, up to `BOOT_TOP` of the way to the knee, found as the first surface seen looking out from the bone (`inside_leg_ring`). The hidden leg above it follows the skirt, the faces fusing boot to pleats are cut, and `add_shins` adds a plain tube up inside the skirt so a lifted boot still has a leg. Hem slivers of 12 vertices or fewer are deleted.
- **Separate head.** A piece above the hips that spans the head bone's height follows the head bone rigidly, and only its bottom `NECK_SHARE`, below the chin, bends into the neck. It shares no volume with the collar, so bone heat can't be trusted on it. Blending all the way up to the head bone left the mouth and chin half on the neck, and a 25° head turn slid them 2.5 cm across the face.
- **Delivered skeleton.** The FBX is one tree of deform bones, which Unity's Humanoid needs: Rigify's own hierarchy fails with `Transform 'DEF-spine' is not an ancestor of 'DEF-thigh.L'`, and the exporter's deform-only option keeps the control parents. The rigged blend keeps its controls and drops the metarig, so `retarget_mocap.py` can read it.
